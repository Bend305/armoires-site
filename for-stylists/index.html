The Pitch.

Styling, without the logistics.

Most styling work is half taste and half freight. Sourcing. Pulling. Steaming. Shipping it back. The part you trained for gets squeezed into whatever time is left over.

On armoires the wardrobe is already digitized, already tagged, already sitting in the app. You open a client's closet, see every piece with its color and fabric and fit, and build looks out of what is there. The freight is gone. What is left is the work you actually wanted.

§01

Set up your studio.

Four decisions, then you are live.

Your tagline

One line on what you are for. This is the first thing a client reads, so make it sound like a person and not a resume.

Your specialties

Tailoring, color, occasion dressing, capsule building, whatever you are actually good at. Clients filter the directory by these.

Your rate

You set the number. It sits on your profile where clients see it before they send you anything, so nobody is negotiating in a DM.

Your capacity

How many clients you want in a week. When you hit it you drop out of the available list. Say no by never being asked.

§02

The work itself.

Brief in, look out. Usually four exchanges.

01

Read the brief.

The occasion, the date, the budget, how they want to feel, and whatever they saved for inspiration. Accept it or pass. Passing costs you nothing except the booking.

02

Build the looks.

Pull from their closet and send a few options. Not one perfect answer. Options, so they can react.

03

Read the votes.

They heart and pass on each option. You see which tags keep winning, which is usually more honest than anything they wrote in the brief.

04

Deliver the winner.

Final look, plus your notes on how to wear it. They can try it on virtually before the day. Then they rate you, and that rating is what the next client sees.

Payouts run on Stripe. You complete Stripe's onboarding once, with a W-9 or W-8 plus your identity and bank details. That information goes to Stripe, not to us. We never see your bank account and neither does your client.

Taxes are yours to file. If your activity crosses the federal or state reporting thresholds for the year, a 1099-K gets issued for you automatically.

There is a platform fee on each booking. The rate is written into your Stylist Agreement and shown to you before you accept a single client. You will never find out what it was after the fact.

Questions.

What stylists ask us.

Do I need my own clients to start?

No. You are listed in the directory and clients find you there. You can also invite people you already style, which is usually how the first month goes.

What does it cost me to be here?

Nothing to set up a studio or to be listed. armoires takes a fee on bookings that actually happen, and that rate is in your agreement before you take a client.

Do I have to shop for anyone?

No, and that is rather the point. You style what is already in the closet. If a client wants a recommendation you can give one, but nothing here is built to push you toward selling.

What if a client is impossible to please?

It happens. The voting step usually catches a mismatch early, before you have sunk an afternoon into it. If it goes sideways after delivery, we can refund the client, and a single refund will not sink your standing. A pattern of them will.

Can I keep working with a client off the app?

Not for work that started here. It is in the agreement, and it is also the deal: we brought you the client and we carry the payment risk, the dispute handling, and the tax paperwork.

How much control do I have over my schedule?

Set your weekly capacity and flip yourself to unavailable whenever you want. You are not penalized for being full. You are penalized for leaving people hanging, which is a different thing.

Who owns the looks I build?

They live in your portfolio and your client's app. Your lookbook is yours to show off, and it is the main thing new clients read before they book you.
